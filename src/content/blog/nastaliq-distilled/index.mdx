[NeuralType](/blog/neuraltype) showed that a font can be a small neural
network instead of a table of outlines, using square Kufic, a style
that lives on a binary grid. This post is the next step: converting an
existing OpenType font into a .ntf neural font. The target is
[Gulzar](https://github.com/googlefonts/Gulzar), an OFL nastaliq by
Borna Izadpanah, Fiona Ross, Alice Savoie, and Simon Cozens.

Nastaliq is the right target because it is the style OpenType limits
the most. Letters in a nastaliq word do not sit side by side on a
baseline; they cascade diagonally, each one placed relative to the
last, and the whole word slopes down to land on the line. OpenType
approximates this with over a thousand glyphs and cursive-attachment
rules. If a neural font is going to matter anywhere, it is here.

The demo below is the pipeline running end to end in your browser: a
1.36M-parameter field model as a 5.4 MB .ntf file, hand-rolled
inference in Rust compiled to WASM, cascade layout, and sub-pixel
contour tracing. It is real text: click, place the cursor, select,
copy, paste. One honest caveat: **the font underneath is the live
training checkpoint**, exported mid-run and re-uploaded as training
proceeds. Early checkpoints draw speckles where letters will be; the
page improves because improving it means overwriting one file. This is
what a build log looks like when the artifact is a neural network.

Reproducing an existing font is not the goal of the NeuralType
project; the goal is fonts that do the manuscript tradition justice,
which no OpenType font does. Distillation is the rehearsal: it forces
every pipeline component the original work will need, on training data
that costs nothing, and it answers the sizing question early. How many
parameters does nastaliq actually need?

### First measurements

Shaping every ordered pair of Arabic letters through Gulzar with
[harfrust](https://github.com/harfbuzz/harfrust) and counting the
distinct glyphs each letter takes depending on its neighbor:

- ن takes 16 different first-position glyphs depending on the letter
  that follows it. ي takes 15. م takes 14.
- Across 31 letters, pairs alone produce 344 distinct first-position
  variants. For comparison, Amiri, a naskh with some of the richest
  contextual behavior in open fonts, produces 150 on the same test.

And the cascade, measured directly: shaping the word نستعليق places
its glyphs at vertical offsets up to **1.38 em above the baseline**.
One word spans more than a full em of vertical travel. That number is
the metal-type box breaking.

A .ntf font does not enumerate variants or attach sorts. The neighbor
is an input, the cascade is generated geometry, and the variants are
what the network learns to produce.

### Extraction

The teacher is Gulzar plus its shaping engine. Given any text,
HarfBuzz consults the font's rule tables and answers: these glyphs, at
these positions. It is deterministic, and it can be asked as many
questions as we want, for free. That is what makes distillation cheap.

The extraction tool (`crates/neuraltype-distill`) asks it every short
question: every single letter, every ordered pair, every ordered
triple, 30,783 small words in all. Triples matter because they are the
shortest text where a letter has a neighbor on both sides, which is
what produces the medial forms. For every letter in every word it
records two things: the composed shape it was drawn with (the base
letterform and its dots, as one unit), and its displacement from the
previous letter. The chain of displacements is the cascade, the entire
nastaliq-specific geometry captured as a list of small vectors.

The dataset: 91,263 letter-in-context records, 411 unique glyphs, and
**1,145 unique composed shapes**. خ alone accounts for over a hundred
of them. Between adjacent letters the cascade drops as much as
1.66 em.

One measurement decided the model's design. With one letter of context
on each side, 358 contexts were ambiguous: the same three letters
could produce different shapes, because Gulzar's rules look further
than that. With two letters of context on each side, the ambiguity is
**zero**. Five letters of context fully determine Gulzar's choice,
which means the network is not approximating a fuzzy relationship. It
is compressing an exact function, the same situation as the square
Kufic font, just three orders of magnitude larger.

### Fields

Each of the 1,145 shapes is rendered to a signed-distance field, a
grayscale grid where the value at each cell is the distance to the
letterform's edge, positive inside and negative outside. This is the
continuous version of NeuralType's binary grid, and it is the
representation the larger project settled on for a reason: a field is
figure and ground, black and white, with no stroke or skeleton
assumptions built in.

Resolution was decided by looking: at 64 pixels per em, Gulzar's
tapers and hairlines survive thresholding; 96 was indistinguishable at
reading size and costs 2.3 times the model outputs. The shared canvas
is 155×219 pixels, large because nastaliq is large: swash tails and
cascade-carried dots extend far from each letter's origin.

### Training

The model is small: five context embeddings feed a multilayer
perceptron, which feeds a stack of five transposed convolutions that
paint the field, plus a two-value head for the displacement. 1.36
million parameters, 5.4 MB in f32, versus Gulzar's 963 KB TTF.
Training runs in [candle](https://github.com/huggingface/candle) so
the same code trains on CPU, Metal, or CUDA; inference in the engine
is hand-rolled Rust with no framework, because the .ntf file is just
weights.

One forensic note from the first night: the identical training run
learns about twenty times faster per epoch on CPU than on candle's
Metal backend, same code, same data. At equal epochs the CPU run had
a contour overlap (IoU) of 0.21 against Metal's 0.01. The overnight
run moved to CPU and the difference was immediate: legible words by
epoch 12.

Build-log status, morning of 2026-07-31: epoch 34 of 100, contour IoU
0.84 and climbing. Thin vertical strokes are the last to form, which
is what the loss predicts: a hairline is worth a few dozen pixels
against the thousands in bowls and letter bodies. A fine-tune pass
with a decaying learning rate follows the main run, the same schedule
trick that took the Kufic font from close to exact.

### Status

1. Extract: **built.** Corpus, cluster records, displacements.
2. Fields: **built.** SDFs at 64 px/em on a shared canvas.
3. Train: **running.** Field plus displacement out; the engine chains
   displacements, which is the cascade.
4. Trace: **built, simple version.** Sub-pixel marching squares;
   [img2bez](/blog/img2bez) is the planned quality pass for
   font-grade bézier outlines once the model's edges deserve them.
5. Measure: held-out IoU per epoch exists; worst-case proof sheets and
   evaluation on real Urdu and Persian text are still to do.
6. Demo: **live above**, same editor island as the NeuralType post.

Amiri, the naskh this post almost targeted, becomes the follow-up:
same pipeline, calmer geometry. The work happens in the
[post-opentype repo](https://github.com/eliheuer/post-opentype); the
plan lives in
[docs/DISTILL.md](https://github.com/eliheuer/post-opentype/blob/main/docs/DISTILL.md).
The distilled font will be released under the OFL with a new name, as
the license requires.
