<p class="draft-notice">This is a build log for work in progress. The conversion described here is not finished and the demo does not exist yet. Like the <a href="/blog/neuraltype">NeuralType post</a>, parts of the text are AI-generated (written with Claude).</p>

[NeuralType](/blog/neuraltype) showed that a font can be a small neural
network instead of a table of outlines, using square Kufic, a style
that lives on a binary grid. This post is the next step: converting an
existing OpenType font, [Amiri](https://github.com/aliftype/amiri),
into a .ntf neural font. Naskh, real curves, real contextual behavior.

Amiri is the right first target. It is an OFL revival of the Bulaq
press naskh with more than a decade of production use and bug fixes,
and it carries some of the richest contextual behavior in open fonts.
After Amiri, the same pipeline should convert
[Gulzar](https://github.com/googlefonts/Gulzar), a nastaliq, where the
real test begins.

Reproducing an existing font is not the goal of the NeuralType
project; the goal is fonts that do the manuscript tradition justice,
which no OpenType font does. Distillation is the rehearsal: it forces
every pipeline component the original work will need, on training data
that costs nothing, and it answers the sizing question early. How many
parameters does contextual naskh actually need?

### First measurement

The four contextual forms (isolated, initial, medial, final) are the
OpenType mental model. Amiri's actual behavior is much richer. Shaping
every ordered pair of Arabic letters through Amiri with
[rustybuzz](https://github.com/harfbuzz/rustybuzz) and counting the
distinct glyphs each letter takes depending on its neighbor:

- ب takes 10 different first-position glyphs depending on the letter
  that follows it. ن and ت and ي: also 10 each.
- ر takes 8 different final-position glyphs depending on the letter
  before it.
- Across 31 letters, pairs alone produce 150 distinct first-position
  variants and 107 second-position variants. Medial contexts will add
  more.

A .ntf font does not enumerate these. The neighbor is an input, and
the variants are what the network learns to produce.

### The plan

1. Extract: shape an all-pairs corpus plus real text through Amiri;
   harvest every glyph-in-context with its outline.
2. Fields: render each shape to a small distance field, the continuous
   version of NeuralType's binary grid.
3. Train: letter, joining form, and neighbor embeddings in; field out.
4. Trace: composite fields per line and trace the result to bézier
   outlines.
5. Measure: held-out fidelity per context, worst cases first.
6. Demo: the same editor island as the NeuralType post, with a font
   picker.

The work happens in the
[post-opentype repo](https://github.com/eliheuer/post-opentype);
the plan lives in
[docs/DISTILL.md](https://github.com/eliheuer/post-opentype/blob/main/docs/DISTILL.md).
The distilled font will be released under the OFL with a new name, as
the license requires.
